\begin{abstract}
Truncation samplers such as top-$p$, min-$p$, and top-$n\sigma$ exist to prevent the
accuracy collapse of high-temperature sampling. We ask whether the collapse itself is
universal. Using a single controlled pipeline, we evaluate 9 instruction-tuned models from
5 model families (1.7B to 12B parameters), 4 quantization levels, 8 sampler arms, and 2
tasks, totaling roughly 200{,}000 generations. At deployment temperatures ($T \le 1.3$),
sampler choice barely matters: outside one family, the per-model accuracy spread across
samplers is 1.3 to 10.0 percentage points (pp), and even that overstates the true
differences. The exception is the Llama family. Under plain temperature sampling at
$T{=}1.3$, Llama-3.1-8B loses 34\,pp (GSM8K) and 41\,pp (MMLU-Pro), Llama-3.2-3B
collapses the same way, and Llama-3-8B loses 17\,pp on MMLU-Pro. Every model outside the
family stays within 8\,pp, including Mistral-7B-v0.3, which is older than Llama-3.1, and
Qwen3-1.7B, whose low-temperature accuracy is close to Llama-3.1's. The
collapse is a termination failure: generations derail midway and never reach an answer,
while accuracy conditional on producing a well-formed answer stays at the greedy level
in every cell (97\% for Llama-3.1-8B on GSM8K at $T{=}1.3$). A temperature ladder up to
$T{=}2.0$ locates each model's cliff and shows that truncation samplers only begin to
matter past it. A logit probe and a forced-token perturbation experiment trace the
fragility to a self-reinforcing instability: one off-distribution token in context
multiplies the fragile model's chance of drawing the next one. Practitioners tuning a
current model can leave the sampler alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Most deployed language models generate text by sampling. At each step the model assigns
a probability to every candidate next token; the decoder either takes the most probable
one (greedy decoding) or draws at random from the distribution. Systems sample because
greedy decoding returns one fixed continuation per prompt, and in open-ended text that
continuation tends to be repetitive \citep{holtzman2020curious}. A temperature parameter
scales the randomness of the draw: raising it moves probability from the model's
preferred tokens toward rare ones.

The rare tokens are where sampling goes wrong. A model's probability estimates are least
reliable in the tail of its distribution, so a high-temperature draw can select a token
that fits the context poorly \citep{holtzman2020curious,hewitt2022truncation}. The
selected token then becomes context for every later step, and the continuation can
degenerate into incoherent text. Truncation samplers exist to prevent this degeneration:
they delete the tail of the distribution before the draw and renormalize what remains.
The rules differ in where they cut the tail; top-$k$, nucleus (top-$p$), min-$p$, and
top-$n\sigma$ sampling are the four we test, and Section~\ref{sec:related} describes each
\citep{fan2018hierarchical,holtzman2020curious,nguyen2024minp,tang2024topn}. All of these
were developed for open-ended generation, such as story writing and dialogue, where
output variety is the goal \citep{zhou2024decoding}.

The same samplers are now evaluated on tasks with one checkable answer. Deployed
assistants keep sampling on for every request, so math word problems and multiple-choice
exams are also decoded at nonzero temperature \citep{song2024goodbad,renze2024temperature}.
On such tasks a decoding method is scored by accuracy: each generation is right or wrong,
where open-ended generation needs a human or model judge \citep{zhou2024decoding}. Min-$p$,
for example, reports accuracy gains over top-$p$ on GSM8K at high temperature
\citep{nguyen2024minp}.

Those accuracy claims drew a critique. \citet{schaeffer2025minp} asked whether any one
sampler beats the others once statistical controls are in place. Whichever sampler wins
that comparison, the comparison itself takes the collapse for granted: it assumes that
raising the temperature eventually ruins every model's accuracy, and asks only which
sampler limits the damage. We test the premise:
\textbf{is the collapse itself a property of decoding, or of the model?}

Across nine instruction-tuned models from five families, run through one controlled
pipeline, the answer is: of the model. The six models from outside the Llama family hold
their accuracy at deployment temperatures under every sampler we test, staying within 8
points of their low-temperature scores. Every larger loss belongs to a Llama:
Llama-3.1-8B and Llama-3.2-3B lose 34 to 41 accuracy points under plain temperature
sampling, and Llama-3-8B loses 17 on the longer task. The collapsing generations derail
into token noise before reaching an answer; the answers these models do complete stay as
accurate as greedy decoding. Sampler choice, the variable the truncation
literature tunes, is worth at most a few points anywhere below each model's collapse
temperature.

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A single-pipeline factorial study.} Seven instruction-tuned models, four
    GGUF quantization levels (model weights stored at reduced precision) plus a BF16
    anchor, six decoding methods plus two ablation arms that swap the order in which
    temperature and truncation are applied, $T \in \{0.7, 1.0, 1.3\}$, on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro with $N{=}50$ items and
    $K{=}3$ repetitions, about 204{,}000 generations on commodity GPUs, with
    item-clustered bootstrap confidence intervals throughout, plus a follow-up
    temperature-only cell on two more Llama generations.
  \item \textbf{Robustness is model-bound, and the fragility runs in one family.}
    Every collapse we observe is a Llama. In the main grid Llama-3.1-8B loses 34 and
    41\,pp at $T{=}1.3$ while Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B, and Gemma-3-12B
    all hold within 8\,pp; in the follow-up cell Llama-3.2-3B collapses the same way and
    Llama-3-8B falls in between (Section~\ref{sec:modelbound}). On the robust models the
    sampler is worth at most a few points, and even that is an overestimate: taking the
    max minus the min of noisy accuracy estimates inflates the range with the noise
    itself, so the true sampler differences are smaller still.
  \item \textbf{The collapse is a termination failure.} Conditional on emitting
    a well-formed final answer, accuracy is flat in temperature everywhere. What varies is
    the probability of derailing into unparseable text mid-generation
    (Section~\ref{sec:degeneration}).
  \item \textbf{A cliff map from $T{=}1.3$ to $2.0$.} Extending the temperature axis
    shows that every model collapses somewhere; what distinguishes models is where. On
    GSM8K, Llama collapses by $T{=}1.5$, Qwen2.5 by 1.7, and Gemma-3 by 2.0. From
    $T{=}1.7$ the separation is statistically clear: the confidence intervals on the
    three models' drops do not overlap one another.
    Truncation samplers shift the cliff right in order of tail-cutting strength
    (top-$n\sigma$ $\gg$ min-$p$ $\gg$ top-$p$). The gains the sampler papers report are
    real, but they appear past the cliff, above deployment temperatures.
  \item \textbf{Supporting results.} Quantization from Q8 down to Q3 causes no collapse
    on any model. It also does not change which sampler ranks best. Its accuracy penalty
    grows as models shrink ($-9\,$pp on Qwen3-1.7B, null at 7B and above). A logit probe, a length-censoring analysis, and a forced-token
    experiment (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}) locate the fragility: every model draws rare
    off-distribution tokens at the same per-token rate, but one injected tail token
    multiplies Llama's subsequent escape hazard 2.3-fold while every robust model absorbs
    it; the size of that multiplier ranks with the collapse across models
    ($\rho = +0.86$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Truncation samplers.}
Top-$k$ sampling draws from only the $k$ most probable tokens; it was introduced for
story generation, where likelihood-maximizing decoding produced generic and repetitive
text \citep{fan2018hierarchical}. \citet{holtzman2020curious} showed that a fixed $k$
fits poorly because the next-token distribution is sometimes peaked and sometimes flat,
and proposed nucleus sampling, which keeps the smallest set of tokens whose probabilities
sum to a target $p$. Min-$p$ keeps only the tokens whose probability is at least a set fraction of the top
token's, so the cut is tight where the model is confident and loose where it is
uncertain \citep{nguyen2024minp}.
Top-$n\sigma$ cuts in logit space instead, keeping tokens within $n$ standard deviations
of the top logit; temperature rescales all logits equally, so the surviving set does not
change with temperature \citep{tang2024topn}. Further refinements of the cut exist, among
them typical sampling and epsilon and eta sampling
\citep{meister2023typical,hewitt2022truncation}; we test the four rules above
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}). All of these rules target the same failure:
incoherent text drawn from the tail at high temperature. \citet{welleck2020consistency}
proved the worst case of that failure, showing that standard decoders can place
probability on generations that never terminate. In contrast to the sampler papers, which
compare truncation rules against each other, we test whether the failure they all guard
against occurs at deployment temperatures at all. Outside the Llama family it does not,
and on the Llamas it takes the non-terminating form: generations run to the token cap
without reaching an answer (Section~\ref{sec:degeneration}).

\paragraph{Reproducibility of sampler gains.}
\citet{schaeffer2025minp} reanalyzed min-$p$ on nine models and 31 temperatures, giving
every sampler an equal number of hyperparameter settings, and found its reported gains
``largely indistinguishable'' from the other samplers'. Part of the gains traced to the
order in which temperature and truncation are applied. The order matters because the
truncation threshold is computed on whichever distribution the rule sees: applied before
temperature, the rule cuts the model's original, sharp distribution; applied after, it
cuts one that temperature has already flattened, so the same nominal setting keeps a
different set of tokens. We adopt both lessons: we report
statistical uncertainty throughout and run the two chain orders as explicit arms
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}). That critique pooled its models together, because its question
was about the sampler. Our question is about the model, so we keep every model separate
and ask whether the collapse happens on each one. \citet{zhou2024decoding} is the closest
systematic sweep of decoding hyperparameters, but it scores open-ended generation with
quality and diversity metrics, while our tasks have one checkable answer.
\citet{song2024goodbad} documents per-model gaps between greedy decoding and sampling on
reasoning benchmarks and treats the gaps as evaluation noise to report. Unlike
\citet{song2024goodbad}, we treat the gap as the object of study, and we trace it to a
termination failure in one model family.

\paragraph{Robustness to decoding randomness.}
\citet{renze2024temperature} sampled temperatures from 0.0 to 1.6 on multiple-choice
problem solving and found no statistically significant effect on accuracy between 0.0 and
1.0, across models, prompting techniques, and domains. Our results replicate that null
inside the deployment band. But the null is pooled across models, and pooling hides the
outlier: our per-model ladder shows the heterogeneity beginning just past $T{=}1.0$,
where Llama-3.1-8B falls while every other model holds (Section~\ref{sec:modelbound}).
The nearest study is \citet{grover2026reliability}, which also asks what makes a model
robust to decoding randomness and concludes that instruction tuning is the primary
determinant. Our data refine that conclusion: all seven of our models are
instruction-tuned and one still collapses, so instruction tuning cannot be the whole
answer. Four design differences let our grid observe what theirs cannot:
\begin{itemize}
  \item their sweep stops at $T{=}1.0$, and the Llama collapse happens between 1.0
    and 1.3;
  \item their robust models score 6 to 30\% on synthetic tasks, so there is little
    accuracy left to lose;
  \item their instruct-versus-base contrast changes quantization at the same time (4-bit
    versus full precision), while we sweep quantization as its own axis and find it is
    not the variable;
  \item they test no truncation samplers.
\end{itemize}
\citet{fastowski2025confidence} connect the entropy of a model's token distributions to
its temperature sensitivity on factual question answering and report that larger models
are more stable. Our logit probe supports the entropy link (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}).
Size does not decide the outcome in our grid, because Qwen3-1.7B holds where the larger
Llama-3.1-8B collapses. We also locate the failure earlier than answer selection: the
fragile model's generations stop terminating, while the answers it does complete stay
accurate (Section~\ref{sec:degeneration}).

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

One pipeline, everything pinned: llama.cpp at a fixed commit (CUDA build), one provider's
imatrix GGUF quantizations with logged SHA256 hashes, official chat templates rendered
client-side with the exact prompt string hashed into each record, the native completion
API, a pinned sampler chain order (temperature first; the llama.cpp default of temperature
last is included as an ablation arm for top-$p$ and min-$p$), per-record derived seeds, a
deterministic greedy anchor ($K{=}1$), and $K{=}3$ repetitions elsewhere.

\textbf{Tasks.} GSM8K (0-shot chain-of-thought, the Llama-eval prompt, numeric exact
match) and MMLU-Pro (0-shot chain-of-thought, letter exact match), $N{=}50$ items each,
with parsers faithful to lm-eval-harness. Both tasks grade a generation by parsing a
final answer out of the generated text, so the parser decides how a generation that never
reaches an answer is graded. The parse path (strict final-answer sentence,
flexible fallback, or failure) is logged per record. Parse failure grades as incorrect
\emph{and} is reported as a metric; Section~\ref{sec:degeneration} shows it is a result,
not a nuisance. One caveat stated up front: the frozen $N{=}50$ MMLU-Pro subset is
head-of-split, and the split is sorted by category, so the subset is single-category
(business). All within-study contrasts hold items fixed; a stratified $N{=}50$ replication
(3--4 items from each of the 14 categories) reproduces the headline result
(Section~\ref{sec:modelbound}, Appendix~\ref{app:strat}).

\textbf{Models and quantization.} Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Qwen2.5-7B,
Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B (thinking off; a thinking-on spot-check in
Appendix~\ref{app:thinking}), and Gemma-3-12B,
all instruction-tuned. Quantization levels Q8\_0, Q6\_K, Q4\_K\_M, Q3\_K\_M, plus a BF16
anchor on Qwen3-4B that matches Q8 within noise.

\textbf{Inference.} Item-clustered nonparametric bootstrap ($B{=}2000$), paired where the
comparison shares items.

\textbf{Hardware.} Generation ran under one pinned llama.cpp commit on two setups: RTX
2080~Ti (11\,GB) nodes of a university Slurm cluster (17 of the 21 main-matrix cells) and
a single RTX 5070~Ti (16\,GB) workstation (the four VRAM-heavy cells and every follow-up
run), with per-architecture CUDA builds of the same commit. About 204{,}000 graded
generations in total. Full reproducibility details in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

The models do not split cleanly into robust and fragile. The Qwen3 family is a tight
null, Qwen2.5, Gemma, and Mistral drop 2 to 8\,pp with at least one task CI excluding
zero, and Llama drops 34 to 41\,pp. Four controls rule out the obvious confounds. It is
not accuracy headroom: Qwen3-1.7B (74\% on GSM8K) sits in Llama's accuracy regime (78\%)
with the opposite temperature behavior. Model age does not explain it either:
Mistral-7B-v0.3 predates Llama-3.1 and is robust. The item subset is not driving it: the
stratified MMLU-Pro replication gives Llama $+43.3\,$pp [31.3, 55.3], Qwen2.5 $+6.0$, and
Gemma $+4.5$. And instruction tuning alone cannot be the answer, since every model here
is instruction-tuned \citep{grover2026reliability}.

The fragility also runs in the lineage. A follow-up cell repeats the pure-temperature
axis on two more Llama generations, Llama-3-8B and Llama-3.2-3B (Q8, same frozen items,
$K{=}3$; Table~\ref{tab:lineage}, Appendix~\ref{app:lineage}). Llama-3.2-3B collapses
like Llama-3.1-8B ($+38.0\,$pp [29.3, 47.3] on GSM8K, $+36.7$ [26.7, 46.0] on MMLU-Pro)
and reproduces the degeneration signature of Section~\ref{sec:degeneration}: the
strict-parse rate falls from 99 to 41\% (GSM8K) and from 83 to 6\% (MMLU-Pro), about
half of all generations run to the token cap, and accuracy among well-formed answers
stays at or above the greedy level. Llama-3-8B sits between the camps: a null on GSM8K
($+4.0$ [$-2.7$, 11.3]) but $+16.7\,$pp [9.3, 24.0] on MMLU-Pro, the longer task, a
larger drop than any non-Llama model shows in Table~\ref{tab:collapse}. Three Llama
generations produce the three largest MMLU-Pro drops in the study, so the fragility is
a family trait; what changed in the family's post-training is the open question of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Sampler choice barely matters before the cliff}

At $T{=}1.3$ the maximum minus minimum accuracy across the seven stochastic sampler arms
is 41.8\,pp (MMLU-Pro) and 36.7\,pp (GSM8K) for Llama, and 1.3 to 10.0\,pp for every other
model (Figure~\ref{fig:spread}). The max-minus-min statistic is biased upward under
sampling noise (the largest and smallest of several noisy estimates land beyond the
largest and smallest true values), so the small spreads are upper bounds; the true
differences on robust models are smaller. Chain order (temperature first versus last) is a no-op on robust
models ($\pm$1--2\,pp). On Llama at $T{=}1.3$ it does matter: temperature-last truncation
is more protective (top-$p$ 64\% $\to$ 73\%, min-$p$ 72\% $\to$ 76\% on GSM8K), as
expected, because truncating before temperature scaling fixes the surviving token set at
its low-temperature size.

\subsection{The collapse is degeneration, not wrong answers}
\label{sec:degeneration}

We decompose accuracy by parse path (Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition}). The drop comes
almost entirely from generations that never finish: at $T{=}1.3$, 53.8\% (GSM8K) and
89.2\% (MMLU-Pro) of Llama generations derail into token-level noise and run to the token
cap. Among the generations that do produce a well-formed final answer, Llama still scores
97.3\% on GSM8K at $T{=}1.3$. That number needs one correction before it can be taken at
face value: survival is selective, because easier items produce shorter generations and
shorter generations have less room to derail. The fair baseline is therefore greedy
decoding restricted to the same surviving items, which scores 95.8\% (against 76.0\% over
all items). Conditional on surviving, then, accuracy at $T{=}1.3$ matches greedy decoding
(97.3 vs.\ 95.8\% on GSM8K; 76.9 vs.\ 78.8\% on MMLU-Pro).

The two tasks disguise one failure mode differently. Degenerate text almost always
contains a number, so the GSM8K flexible last-number fallback grades it as a wrong answer;
the MMLU-Pro letter parser reports it as a parse failure. The underlying event is the same
on both tasks: the generation derails before it reaches an answer.

\subsection{The cliff map: robustness is cliff location}
\label{sec:cliff}

We extend the temperature axis to $T \in \{1.3, 1.5, 1.7, 2.0\}$ on three models spanning
the gradient (Q8, both tasks, $N{=}50$, $K{=}3$; Figure~\ref{fig:cliff}). Under plain
temperature on GSM8K the three models form a staircase, one rung apart: Llama falls 45
$\to$ 9 $\to$ 1 $\to$ 0\%, Qwen2.5 falls 89 $\to$ 69 $\to$ 23 $\to$ 2\%, and Gemma falls
89 $\to$ 77 $\to$ 49 $\to$ 8\%. The drops against each model's own $T{=}0.7$ anchor
order the staircase at every rung: at $T{=}1.5$ Llama ($+68.0\,$pp [58.0, 76.7]) is
disjoint from both Qwen2.5 ($+24.0$ [15.3, 33.3]) and Gemma ($+14.7$ [8.7, 21.3]), whose
intervals still touch; by $T{=}1.7$ all three are pairwise disjoint (Qwen2.5 $+70.0$
[62.0, 77.3] versus Gemma $+42.7$ [34.7, 50.7]). Every model has a cliff; a robust model
simply has it later, roughly $0.2$ in temperature per step of the gradient. Within each
model the cliff sits earlier on MMLU-Pro, the task with roughly twice the generation
length; this is what a compounding per-token derail risk predicts. There Llama is at 3\%
already at $T{=}1.3$, Qwen2.5 falls 57 $\to$ 25 $\to$ 4 $\to$ 0\%, and Gemma falls 60
$\to$ 24 $\to$ 6 $\to$ 0\%. The longer task also compresses the
staircase: Qwen2.5 and Gemma land on the same MMLU-Pro rung ($T{=}1.5$) while remaining
one rung apart on GSM8K.

Truncation shifts the cliff right in order of tail-cutting strength. Top-$p$ buys about
0.2 in temperature. Min-$p$ holds 70\% at $T{=}1.5$ on Llama GSM8K where plain temperature
is at 9\%, 85--87\% on Qwen2.5 and Gemma GSM8K out to $T{=}2.0$, and 53 to 59\% on Qwen
MMLU-Pro. Top-$n\sigma$ is flat to $T{=}2.0$ on all three models (75 to 93\% on GSM8K; 56
to 66\% on Qwen MMLU-Pro), consistent with its construction: the surviving token set is
temperature-invariant. The strict-parse rate tracks accuracy almost one-to-one along the
whole ladder, so the cliff \emph{is} the degeneration cliff. This reconciles the sampler
papers with our null result: min-$p$ and top-$n\sigma$ validate at $T \ge 1.5$ to 3, past
every model's deployment range. There the gains are real and ordered top-$n\sigma$ $\gg$
min-$p$ $\gg$ top-$p$ (the $T{=}2$--3 probe in Appendix~\ref{app:hight}), but at $T \le
1.3$ on a robust
model the samplers have no failure left to prevent.

\subsection{Quantization and self-consistency}

Greedy accuracy from Q8\_0 to Q3\_K\_M (pooled tasks) is null on every model of 7B
parameters or more (Gemma $-3.0$ [$-9$, $+3$]) and $-9\,$pp on Qwen3-1.7B: the effect
grows as models shrink, and nowhere does it reorder samplers
(Figure~\ref{fig:quant}). Self-consistency at $T{=}1.3$: robust models emit three distinct
token sequences across $K{=}3$ repetitions yet converge to 1.1 to 1.4 distinct parsed
answers per item; Llama and Mistral diverge to 2.2 to 2.5.

\subsection{Toward a mechanism: per-token uncertainty ranks with fragility}
\label{sec:mechanism}

If the collapse is a derailment process, fragile models should carry more per-token
uncertainty before any derailment happens. We probe this directly: for every model (Q8)
we decode the frozen 100 prompts greedily and record the raw top-20 next-token
probabilities at each position, summarized per prompt as an entropy lower bound, the
top-1 probability, the top-1 minus top-2 margin, and the fraction of low-confidence
positions (top-1 $<$ 0.5). Greedy decoding holds the trajectory at the model's modal
path, so the measurement is not confounded by sampled derailments.

Mean greedy-path entropy ranks with the observed temperature drop at Spearman
$\rho = +0.96$ across the seven models (flat-position fraction $+0.96$; top-1 margin
$-0.96$; Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}). The ranking is informative and so is its failure:
Llama-3.1 (entropy 0.33 nats) and Mistral (0.32) are nearly tied on every top-20 summary,
including the mass beyond the top 20 ($1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ both), yet their drops differ
five-fold (40.8 versus 8.0\,pp).

Generation lengths rule out one candidate mechanism: derail-and-recover. If models
derailed and recovered, terminated generations at $T{=}1.3$ would carry the extra tokens
of the detour. They do not: on every robust model the terminated-length distribution is
essentially unchanged from $T{=}0.7$ (90th-percentile ratio 0.85 to 1.19 across models
and tasks; Table~\ref{tab:recovery}).
Llama's surviving generations at $T{=}1.3$ are \emph{shorter} than at $T{=}0.7$ (90th
percentile $\times 0.73$ on GSM8K, $\times 0.55$ on MMLU-Pro): exactly the censoring an
absorbing failure with a compounding per-token hazard produces, where the generations
that survive are the ones short enough to finish before the first bad draw. Derailment
is an absorbing state in every model.

We also estimated the hazard directly. Re-decoding the frozen prompts and storing the
raw top-20 arrays at every position, we compute, for each position, the probability that
sampling at $T{=}1.3$ picks a token from beyond the model's own top 20:
0.0033 per position for Llama versus 0.0034 for Mistral (about 0.85 expected tail draws
per 256 positions for both; across all seven models this hazard ranks with the drop at
the same $\rho = +0.96$). Both models take the first off-distribution token equally
often; only Llama spirals on it. A single absorbed token leaves no length footprint, so
this is consistent with the unchanged terminated lengths above. Whatever separates the
two models is not visible in the next-token distribution, scaled or unscaled. That leaves
one candidate: how the model behaves after an off-distribution token has entered its own
context.

A perturbation experiment measures that instability directly. For each model and frozen
prompt we greedy-decode a 48-token prefix, force the model's own rank-20 candidate at
that position (median raw probability $2 \times 10^{-9}$: a genuine tail draw), and
continue greedily for 256 tokens against a control continuation of the unedited prefix.
On the surface the forced token is absorbed: continuations stay locally coherent, and
Llama's reach the token cap no more often than its controls (14.0\% both, GSM8K). The
escape hazard says otherwise. Over the 32 positions after the injection, Llama's
beyond-top-20 escape hazard at $T{=}1.3$ rises to 2.3 times its baseline ($+0.0042$ per
position); Mistral's rises 1.35 times ($+0.0012$), and no robust model gains more than
$+0.0014$. The per-model destabilization ranks with the observed temperature drop at
$\rho = +0.86$ (entropy delta: $+0.82$). This is the compounding loop measured directly:
one off-distribution token in context multiplies the probability of drawing the next
one, and what separates the fragile model is the multiplier, not the base rate
(Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism} consolidates all per-model probe numbers). With seven
models these are consistency checks, not a confirmed causal account, and we report them
as such.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{For practitioners.} Pick the model, not the sampler. Sampler tuning is insurance
against a failure mode that current post-training has largely removed at deployment
temperatures.

\textbf{For the sampler literature.} Truncation samplers solve a real but receding
problem. Their standard evaluation regime ($T \ge 2$) measures the size of the safety
margin, not deployment gains. Top-$n\sigma$'s temperature invariance does exactly what it
promises (flat to $T{=}2.0$ on all three ladder models), but the benefit only appears
past the cliff.

\textbf{For the robustness line.} Instruction tuning does not explain the split: all
seven models here are instruction-tuned. What changed between Llama-3.1 and the 2025
cohort (post-training recipe, data, RLHF) is an open question; we contribute the
behavioral signature a mechanism study should explain: termination failure with intact
answer selection, set off by off-distribution tokens that arrive at the same per-token
rate in every model but destabilize only one.

\textbf{Why degeneration matters beyond benchmarks.} An answer-selection failure and a
derailment failure have different production consequences (silent errors versus detectable
garbage) and different fixes (output validation versus sampler guard rails).

\section{Conclusion}
Across seven models, five samplers, four quantization levels, and two reasoning tasks, we
find that the choice of sampler is a second-order effect at deployment temperatures: on
robust models the max-minus-min accuracy spread across samplers is 1.3 to 10.0 points at
$T{=}1.3$, while a single model, Llama-3.1-8B, loses 34 to 41 points under pure
temperature on the same grid. The collapse is a termination failure: conditional on
producing a well-formed answer, accuracy stays at the greedy level in every cell. Every
model has a cliff above its deployment range, and robustness is where that cliff sits.
Truncation samplers move the cliff to higher temperatures, but the gains begin above
deployment settings. Per-token entropy on the greedy decoding path ranks models by
fragility (Spearman $\rho{=}+0.96$ across the seven models), and a single forced tail
token multiplies the fragile model's subsequent escape hazard 2.3-fold where robust
models absorb it. Together these results argue for screening a model's temperature
fragility before deployment rather than tuning the sampler after it. The open question
our data sharpen but cannot answer is what, in post-training, moves the cliff.

\section*{Limitations}

$N{=}50$ items per task (CIs are shown throughout; the headline effects are 5 to 10 times
their CI widths). 0-shot prompting, English, two tasks. The MMLU-Pro head subset is
single-category; the stratified replication is in Appendix~\ref{app:strat}. Every fragile
model we observe is a Llama: the lineage cell (Section~\ref{sec:modelbound}) shows the
fragility spans three Llama generations, so it is a family trait, but with one fragile
family we cannot say how common such families are. Mistral is near floor on GSM8K (32.7\% at $T{=}0.7$),
which limits its collapse-detection power there (its MMLU-Pro CI excludes zero). The
max-minus-min spread is biased upward, which works against our claim. Accuracy conditional
on strict parsing has item survivorship, quantified and normalized in
Section~\ref{sec:degeneration}. llama.cpp k-quants only, one quantization recipe by
design. Thinking mode off by default (the thinking-on spot-check in
Appendix~\ref{app:thinking} agrees).

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Inference stack.} llama.cpp at commit \texttt{5aba5364} (server build
    9456), native \texttt{/completion} endpoint, CUDA builds of the same commit per GPU
    architecture (sm\_75 for RTX 2080~Ti, sm\_120 for RTX 5070~Ti). Server-side BOS
    insertion is disabled; the client-rendered chat template owns the special tokens, and
    each record stores the SHA-256 of the exact rendered prompt.
  \item \textbf{Quantizations.} Every quantized checkpoint is a Bartowski imatrix GGUF
    (one provider, one recipe, so the quantization axis is not confounded by recipe).
    SHA-256 hashes of all 29 model files are pinned in the repository
    (\texttt{DOWNLOADS.md} and per-file \texttt{.meta.json} sidecars).
  \item \textbf{Decoding.} Exactly one truncation parameter per request; the sampler
    chain order is pinned per arm and recorded in every record. Sampler values: top-$p$
    0.95, top-$k$ 40, min-$p$ 0.05, top-$n\sigma$ 1.0 (each method's recommended value).
  \item \textbf{Seeding.} Every generation's seed is derived deterministically from
    (global seed 0, model, quantization, sampler, temperature, task, item, repetition).
    Greedy decoding is the deterministic $T{=}0$ anchor with $K{=}1$.
  \item \textbf{Tasks.} GSM8K (openai/gsm8k, test split, first 50 items) and MMLU-Pro
    (TIGER-Lab/MMLU-Pro, test split, first 50 items; stratified replication in
    Appendix~\ref{app:strat}). Parsers mirror lm-eval-harness; parse failures grade as
    incorrect and are reported.
  \item \textbf{Records.} Each generation is one JSONL record carrying the full identity
    tuple, the request parameters, the raw output, the parse path, and throughput
    covariates; runs are resumable by record id. Batched serving ($\le 8$ slots) is
    seed-logged but not bit-exact.

\end{itemize}
