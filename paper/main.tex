\begin{abstract}
Truncation samplers such as top-$p$, min-$p$, and top-$n\sigma$ exist to prevent the
accuracy collapse of high-temperature sampling. We ask whether the collapse itself is
universal. Using a single controlled pipeline, we evaluate 7 instruction-tuned models from
5 vendors (1.7B to 12B parameters), 4 quantization levels, 8 sampler arms, and 2 tasks,
totaling roughly 200{,}000 generations. At deployment temperatures ($T \le 1.3$), sampler
choice barely matters on six of the seven models: the per-model accuracy spread across
samplers is 1.3 to 10.0 percentage points (pp), an upper bound by construction. The exception is Llama-3.1-8B, which loses
34\,pp (GSM8K) and 41\,pp (MMLU-Pro) under plain temperature sampling at $T{=}1.3$, while
every other model, including the older Mistral-7B-v0.3 and the accuracy-matched
Qwen3-1.7B, stays within 8\,pp. The collapse is a failure to terminate, not a failure to
reason: accuracy conditional on producing a well-formed answer stays at the greedy level
in every cell (97\% for Llama on GSM8K at $T{=}1.3$). A temperature ladder up to
$T{=}2.0$ locates each model's cliff and shows that truncation samplers earn their keep
only past it. A logit probe and a forced-token perturbation experiment trace the
fragility to a self-reinforcing instability: one off-distribution token in context
multiplies the fragile model's chance of drawing the next one. Practitioners tuning a
current model can leave the sampler alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The truncation-sampler literature, from top-$k$ and nucleus sampling to min-$p$ and
top-$n\sigma$ \citep{fan2018hierarchical,holtzman2020curious,nguyen2024minp,tang2024topn},
is motivated by a single failure mode: raise the sampling temperature and output quality
collapses. A recent critique \citep{schaeffer2025minp} questioned whether the reported gains
of newer samplers survive proper statistical controls. We ask the prior question:
\textbf{is the collapse itself a property of decoding, or of the model?}

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A single-pipeline factorial study.} Seven instruction-tuned models, four
    GGUF quantization levels plus a BF16 anchor, six decoding methods plus two chain-order
    ablation arms, $T \in \{0.7, 1.0, 1.3\}$, on GSM8K and MMLU-Pro with $N{=}50$ items and
    $K{=}3$ repetitions, about 202{,}000 generations on commodity GPUs (RTX 2080~Ti
    cluster nodes and one RTX 5070~Ti workstation), with item-clustered bootstrap
    confidence intervals throughout.
  \item \textbf{Robustness is model-bound.} Llama-3.1-8B is the lone collapser
    ($-34/-41\,$pp at $T{=}1.3$). Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B, and
    Gemma-3-12B all hold within 8\,pp. On the robust models the sampler is worth at most a
    few points, and that estimate is an upper bound because the spread statistic is biased
    upward.
  \item \textbf{The collapse is degeneration, not wrong answers.} Conditional on emitting
    a well-formed final answer, accuracy is flat in temperature everywhere. What varies is
    the probability of derailing into unparseable text mid-generation. The standard GSM8K
    flexible parser disguises this failure mode as wrong answers; the MMLU-Pro letter
    parser reports it as parse failure.
  \item \textbf{A cliff map from $T{=}1.3$ to $2.0$.} Every model has a cliff; its
    location is the model property, a staircase one rung apart (on GSM8K: Llama collapses
    by $T{=}1.5$, Qwen2.5 by 1.7, Gemma-3 by 2.0; all three pairwise disjoint CIs by 1.7).
    Truncation samplers shift the cliff right in order of tail-cutting strength
    (top-$n\sigma$ $\gg$ min-$p$ $\gg$ top-$p$). This reconciles our null with the sampler
    papers: their gains are real but live past the cliff, where no one deploys.
  \item \textbf{Supporting results.} Quantization (Q8 to Q3) neither causes a collapse nor
    reorders samplers; its effect grows as models shrink ($-9\,$pp on Qwen3-1.7B, null at
    7B and above). Self-consistency shows robust models sample diverse token sequences
    that converge to the same answer. A logit probe on each model's greedy path finds
    per-token uncertainty ranks with fragility (Spearman $\rho = 0.96$); generation-length
    censoring shows derailment is absorbing (no model derails and recovers); Llama and
    Mistral tie on every distributional statistic, including the temperature-scaled
    beyond-top-20 hazard; and a forced-token experiment isolates the difference: one
    injected tail token multiplies Llama's subsequent escape hazard 2.3-fold while every
    robust model absorbs it ($\rho = +0.86$ with the drop).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Truncation samplers.}
Top-$k$ \citep{fan2018hierarchical} and nucleus sampling \citep{holtzman2020curious} were
introduced to prevent \emph{neural text degeneration}: incoherent continuations caused by
sampling from the unreliable tail of the distribution. Successors refine the truncation
rule: typical sampling \citep{meister2023typical}, eta and epsilon sampling framed as
desmoothing \citep{hewitt2022truncation}, the confidence-scaled threshold of min-$p$
\citep{nguyen2024minp}, and the logit-space cut of top-$n\sigma$, temperature-invariant by
construction \citep{tang2024topn}. \citet{welleck2020consistency} formalized the limiting
case of degeneration: standard decoders can fail to terminate at all, a failure mode of
the decoding process rather than of answer selection. Our mechanism result closes a loop
with this lineage: the high-temperature \emph{accuracy} collapse on reasoning benchmarks
is exactly this failure (generations that run to the token cap, not wrong answers), and on
most current models it has moved past deployment temperatures.

\paragraph{Reproducibility of sampler gains.}
The min-$p$ critique \citep{schaeffer2025minp} reanalyzed nine models across 31 temperatures
and found min-$p$ ``largely indistinguishable'' from other samplers once hyperparameter
volume is equalized, tracing part of the reported gains to a change in temperature
ordering. We adopt its methodological recommendations (statistical uncertainty, explicit
temperature-first and temperature-last arms) and answer the question it left open: it
pooled across models to ask whether min-$p$ is special (it is not); we ask whether the
collapse the samplers exist to fix is itself model-dependent (it is).
\citet{zhou2024decoding} is the closest systematic hyperparameter sweep, on open-ended
generation rather than verifiable tasks. \citet{song2024goodbad} documents per-model gaps
between greedy decoding and sampling but frames them as evaluation noise to report, not as
a model property to explain.

\paragraph{Robustness to decoding randomness.}
\citet{renze2024temperature} sampled temperatures from 0.0 to 1.6 on multiple-choice
problem solving and found no statistically significant effect between 0.0 and 1.0,
across models, prompting techniques, and domains. Our deployment-band null replicates
this, and our ladder explains why the null is incomplete: the per-model heterogeneity
starts just past where pooled analyses stop looking, and pooling hides the one model
that falls early. The nearest neighbor is \citet{grover2026reliability}, which concludes that instruction
tuning is the primary determinant of robustness to decoding randomness. Our data refine
this claim: all seven of our models are instruction-tuned and one still collapses, so
instruction tuning is necessary but not sufficient. Their design also cannot observe our
phenomenon: they cap the sweep at $T{=}1.0$ while the Llama collapse happens between 1.0
and 1.3; their robust models score 6 to 30\% on synthetic tasks, so there is little room
to fall; their instruct-versus-base contrast is confounded with 4-bit versus
full-precision inference, whereas we sweep a clean quantization axis and find quantization
is not the variable; and they test no truncation samplers.
\citet{fastowski2025confidence} connect token-distribution entropy to temperature
sensitivity on closed-book QA and report that larger models are more stable. Our data
support the entropy link but not the size story: Qwen3-1.7B is robust where Llama-3.1-8B
collapses, and we further show the instability is termination failure rather than factual
drift. A related test-time-scaling line attributes temperature-flattening in reasoning
models to internal deliberation; our robust models flatten with thinking mode off (a
thinking-on spot-check agrees), so deliberation is not the mechanism here.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

One pipeline, everything pinned: llama.cpp at a fixed commit (CUDA build), one provider's
imatrix GGUF quantizations with logged SHA256 hashes, official chat templates rendered
client-side with the exact prompt string hashed into each record, the native completion
API, a pinned sampler chain order (temperature first; the llama.cpp default of temperature
last is included as an ablation arm for top-$p$ and min-$p$), per-record derived seeds, a
deterministic greedy anchor ($K{=}1$), and $K{=}3$ repetitions elsewhere.

\textbf{Tasks.} GSM8K (0-shot chain-of-thought, the Llama-eval prompt, numeric exact
match) and MMLU-Pro (0-shot chain-of-thought, letter exact match), $N{=}50$ items each,
with parsers faithful to lm-eval-harness. The parse path (strict final-answer sentence,
flexible fallback, or failure) is logged per record. Parse failure grades as incorrect
\emph{and} is reported as a metric; Section~\ref{sec:degeneration} shows it is a result,
not a nuisance. One caveat stated up front: the frozen $N{=}50$ MMLU-Pro subset is
head-of-split, and the split is sorted by category, so the subset is single-category
(business). All within-study contrasts hold items fixed; a stratified $N{=}50$ replication
(3--4 items from each of the 14 categories) reproduces the headline result
(Section~\ref{sec:modelbound}, Appendix~C).

\textbf{Models and quantization.} Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Qwen2.5-7B,
Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B (thinking off; a thinking-on spot-check in Appendix~F), and Gemma-3-12B,
all instruction-tuned. Quantization levels Q8\_0, Q6\_K, Q4\_K\_M, Q3\_K\_M, plus a BF16
anchor on Qwen3-4B that matches Q8 within noise.

\textbf{Inference.} Item-clustered nonparametric bootstrap ($B{=}2000$), paired where the
comparison shares items.

\textbf{Hardware.} Generation ran under one pinned llama.cpp commit on two setups: RTX
2080~Ti (11\,GB) nodes of a university Slurm cluster (17 of the 21 main-matrix cells) and
a single RTX 5070~Ti (16\,GB) workstation (the four VRAM-heavy cells and every follow-up
run), with per-architecture CUDA builds of the same commit. About 202{,}000 graded
generations in total. Full reproducibility details in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

The effect is a gradient, not a binary: the Qwen3 family is a tight null, Qwen2.5, Gemma,
and Mistral show small but real drops, and Llama is catastrophic. Four controls rule out
the obvious confounds. It is not accuracy headroom: Qwen3-1.7B (74\% on GSM8K) sits in
Llama's accuracy regime (78\%) with the opposite temperature behavior. It is not model
age: Mistral-7B-v0.3 predates Llama-3.1 and is robust. It is not the item subset: the
stratified MMLU-Pro replication gives Llama $+43.3\,$pp [31.3, 55.3], Qwen2.5 $+6.0$, and
Gemma $+4.5$. And it is not instruction tuning alone, since every model here is
instruction-tuned \citep{grover2026reliability}.

\subsection{Sampler choice barely matters before the cliff}

At $T{=}1.3$ the maximum minus minimum accuracy across the seven stochastic sampler arms
is 41.8\,pp (MMLU-Pro) and 36.7\,pp (GSM8K) for Llama, and 1.3 to 10.0\,pp for every other
model (Figure~\ref{fig:spread}). The max-minus-min statistic is biased upward under
sampling noise, so the small spreads are upper bounds; the true differences on robust
models are smaller. Chain order (temperature first versus last) is a no-op on robust
models ($\pm$1--2\,pp) and matters only where everything matters: on Llama at $T{=}1.3$,
temperature-last truncation is more protective (top-$p$ 64\% $\to$ 73\%, min-$p$ 72\% $\to$
76\% on GSM8K), as expected, because truncating before temperature scaling fixes the
surviving token set at its low-temperature size.

\subsection{The collapse is degeneration, not wrong answers}
\label{sec:degeneration}

We decompose accuracy by parse path (Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition}). What collapses is
termination: at $T{=}1.3$, 53.8\% (GSM8K) and 89.2\% (MMLU-Pro) of Llama generations
derail into token-level noise and run to the token cap. Among generations that do produce
a well-formed final answer, Llama scores 97.3\% on GSM8K at $T{=}1.3$. Survival is
selective (harder items derail first: greedy accuracy on the surviving items is 95.8\%,
against 76.0\% over all items), so the properly normalized statement is that,
\emph{conditional on surviving, accuracy at $T{=}1.3$ matches greedy decoding on the same
items} (97.3 vs.\ 95.8\% on GSM8K; 76.9 vs.\ 78.8\% on MMLU-Pro). Temperature's entire
marginal effect is on whether the generation survives, not on which answer it picks.

The two tasks disguise one failure mode differently. Degenerate text almost always
contains a number, so the GSM8K flexible last-number fallback grades it as a wrong answer;
the MMLU-Pro letter parser reports it as a parse failure. Temperature does not make a
model choose worse answers; it makes a fragile model fail to finish.

\subsection{The cliff map: robustness is cliff location}
\label{sec:cliff}

We extend the temperature axis to $T \in \{1.3, 1.5, 1.7, 2.0\}$ on three models spanning
the gradient (Q8, both tasks, $N{=}50$, $K{=}3$; Figure~\ref{fig:cliff}). Under plain
temperature on GSM8K the three models form a staircase, one rung apart: Llama falls 45
$\to$ 9 $\to$ 1 $\to$ 0\%, Qwen2.5 falls 89 $\to$ 69 $\to$ 23 $\to$ 2\%, and Gemma falls
89 $\to$ 77 $\to$ 49 $\to$ 8\%. The drops against each model's own $T{=}0.7$ anchor
order the staircase at every rung: at $T{=}1.5$ Llama ($+68.0\,$pp [58.0, 76.7]) is
disjoint from both Qwen2.5 ($+24.0$ [15.3, 33.3]) and Gemma ($+14.7$ [8.7, 21.3]), whose
intervals still touch; by $T{=}1.7$ all three are pairwise disjoint (Qwen2.5 $+70.0$
[62.0, 77.3] versus Gemma $+42.7$ [34.7, 50.7]). Every model has a cliff; what a robust
model owns is the cliff's location, roughly $0.2$ in temperature per step of the
gradient. Within each model the cliff sits earlier on MMLU-Pro (Llama is at 3\% already
at $T{=}1.3$; Qwen2.5 falls 57 $\to$ 25 $\to$ 4 $\to$ 0\%; Gemma falls 60 $\to$ 24 $\to$
6 $\to$ 0\%), the task with roughly twice the generation length, which is what a
compounding per-token derail risk predicts. The longer task also compresses the
staircase: Qwen2.5 and Gemma land on the same MMLU-Pro rung ($T{=}1.5$) while remaining
one rung apart on GSM8K.

Truncation shifts the cliff right in order of tail-cutting strength. Top-$p$ buys about
0.2 in temperature. Min-$p$ holds 70\% at $T{=}1.5$ on Llama GSM8K where plain temperature
is at 9\%, 85--87\% on Qwen2.5 and Gemma GSM8K out to $T{=}2.0$, and 53 to 59\% on Qwen
MMLU-Pro. Top-$n\sigma$ is flat to $T{=}2.0$ on all three models (75 to 93\% on GSM8K; 56
to 66\% on Qwen MMLU-Pro), consistent with its construction: the surviving token set is
temperature-invariant. The strict-parse rate tracks accuracy almost one-to-one along the
whole ladder, so the cliff \emph{is} the degeneration cliff. This reconciles the sampler
papers with our null result: min-$p$ and top-$n\sigma$ validate at $T \ge 1.5$ to 3, past
every model's deployment range. There the gains are real and ordered top-$n\sigma$ $\gg$
min-$p$ $\gg$ top-$p$ (the $T{=}2$--3 probe in Appendix~D), but at $T \le 1.3$ on a robust
model there is nothing left for a sampler to fix.

\subsection{Quantization and self-consistency}

Greedy accuracy from Q8\_0 to Q3\_K\_M (pooled tasks) is null on every model of 7B
parameters or more (Gemma $-3.0$ [$-9$, $+3$]) and $-9\,$pp on Qwen3-1.7B: the effect
grows as models shrink, and nowhere does it reorder samplers
(Figure~\ref{fig:quant}). Self-consistency at $T{=}1.3$: robust models emit three distinct
token sequences across $K{=}3$ repetitions yet converge to 1.1 to 1.4 distinct parsed
answers per item; Llama and Mistral diverge to 2.2 to 2.5. Robustness is convergence in
answer space, not a peaked next-token distribution.

\subsection{Toward a mechanism: per-token uncertainty ranks with fragility}
\label{sec:mechanism}

If the collapse is a derailment process, fragile models should carry more per-token
uncertainty before any derailment happens. We probe this directly: for every model (Q8)
we decode the frozen 100 prompts greedily and record the raw top-20 next-token
probabilities at each position, summarized per prompt as an entropy lower bound, the
top-1 probability, the top-1 minus top-2 margin, and the fraction of low-confidence
positions (top-1 $<$ 0.5). Greedy decoding holds the trajectory at the model's modal
path, so the measurement is not confounded by sampled derailments.

Mean greedy-path entropy ranks with the observed temperature drop at Spearman
$\rho = +0.96$ across the seven models (flat-position fraction $+0.96$; top-1 margin
$-0.96$; Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}). The ranking is informative and so is its failure:
Llama-3.1 (entropy 0.33 nats) and Mistral (0.32) are nearly tied on every top-20 summary,
including the mass beyond the top 20 ($1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ both), yet their drops differ
five-fold (40.8 versus 8.0\,pp).

Generation lengths say where the missing mechanism is not. If models derailed and
recovered, terminated generations at $T{=}1.3$ would carry the extra tokens of the
detour; instead, on every robust model the terminated-length distribution is essentially
unchanged from $T{=}0.7$ (90th-percentile ratio 0.85 to 1.19 across models and tasks;
Table~\ref{tab:recovery}).
Llama's surviving generations at $T{=}1.3$ are \emph{shorter} than at $T{=}0.7$ (90th
percentile $\times 0.73$ on GSM8K, $\times 0.55$ on MMLU-Pro): exactly the censoring an
absorbing failure with a compounding per-token hazard produces, where the generations
that survive are the ones short enough to finish before the first bad draw. Derailment
is an absorbing state in every model.

A direct estimate of the hazard itself closes the remaining door. Re-decoding the frozen
prompts and storing the raw top-20 arrays at every position, we compute the
temperature-scaled probability of drawing a token from beyond the top 20 at $T{=}1.3$:
0.0033 per position for Llama versus 0.0034 for Mistral (about 0.85 expected tail draws
per 256 positions for both; across all seven models this hazard ranks with the drop at
the same $\rho = +0.96$). Both models take the first off-distribution token equally
often; only Llama spirals on it. A single absorbed token leaves no length footprint, so
this is consistent with the unchanged terminated lengths above. The fragility is
therefore not in the next-token distribution at all, scaled or unscaled: it is the
model's instability to a single off-distribution token in its own context.

A perturbation experiment measures that instability directly. For each model and frozen
prompt we greedy-decode a 48-token prefix, force the model's own rank-20 candidate at
that position (median raw probability $2 \times 10^{-9}$: a genuine tail draw), and
continue greedily for 256 tokens against a control continuation of the unedited prefix.
The forced token is absorbed on the surface: continuations stay locally coherent, and
Llama's reach the token cap no more often than its controls (14.0\% both, GSM8K). The
destabilization is underneath. Over the 32 positions after the injection, Llama's
beyond-top-20 escape hazard at $T{=}1.3$ rises to 2.3 times its baseline ($+0.0042$ per
position); Mistral's rises 1.35 times ($+0.0012$), and no robust model gains more than
$+0.0014$. The per-model destabilization ranks with the observed temperature drop at
$\rho = +0.86$ (entropy delta: $+0.82$). This is the compounding loop measured directly:
one off-distribution token in context multiplies the probability of drawing the next
one, and what separates the fragile model is the multiplier, not the base rate
(Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism} consolidates all per-model probe numbers). With seven
models these are consistency checks, not a confirmed causal account, and we report them
as such.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{For practitioners.} Pick the model, not the sampler. Sampler tuning is insurance
against a failure mode that current post-training has largely removed at deployment
temperatures.

\textbf{For the sampler literature.} Truncation samplers solve a real but receding
problem. Their standard evaluation regime ($T \ge 2$) measures the size of the safety
margin, not deployment gains. Top-$n\sigma$'s temperature invariance does exactly what it
promises, but one has to stand past the cliff to see it.

\textbf{For the robustness line.} The variable is the model, not instruction tuning. What
changed between Llama-3.1 and the 2025 cohort (post-training recipe, data, RLHF) is an
open question; we contribute the behavioral signature a mechanism study should explain:
termination failure with intact answer selection, triggered by off-distribution tokens
that arrive at the same per-token rate in robust models and are absorbed only by them.

\textbf{Why degeneration matters beyond benchmarks.} An answer-selection failure and a
derailment failure have different production consequences (silent errors versus detectable
garbage) and different fixes (output validation versus sampler guard rails).

\section{Limitations}

$N{=}50$ items per task (CIs are shown throughout; the headline effects are 5 to 10 times
their CI widths). 0-shot prompting, English, two tasks. The MMLU-Pro head subset is
single-category; the stratified replication is in Appendix~C. One fragile model: we
characterize Llama-3.1, not the class of fragile models, and whether fragility is
lineage-specific is untested. Mistral is near floor on GSM8K (32.7\% at $T{=}0.7$), which
limits its collapse-detection power there (its MMLU-Pro CI excludes zero). The
max-minus-min spread is biased upward, which works against our claim. Accuracy conditional
on strict parsing has item survivorship, quantified and normalized in
Section~\ref{sec:degeneration}. llama.cpp k-quants only, one quantization recipe by
design. Thinking mode off by default (the thinking-on spot-check in Appendix~F agrees).

\section{Conclusion}
Across seven models, five samplers, four quantization levels, and two reasoning tasks, we
find that the choice of sampler is a second-order effect at deployment temperatures: on
robust models the max-minus-min accuracy spread across samplers is 1.3 to 10.0 points at
$T{=}1.3$, while a single model, Llama-3.1-8B, loses 34 to 41 points under pure
temperature on the same grid. The collapse is not an answer-selection failure but a
termination failure, every model has such a cliff somewhere above its deployment range,
and what the literature calls robustness is the location of that cliff; truncation
samplers shift it rightward but pay off only at temperatures nobody deploys. Per-token
entropy on the greedy decoding path ranks models by fragility (Spearman $\rho{=}+0.96$
across the seven models), and a single forced tail token multiplies the fragile model's
subsequent escape hazard 2.3-fold where robust models absorb it, which together suggest
cheap screening before deployment rather than sampler tuning after it. The open question our data sharpen but cannot answer is what, in
post-training, moves the cliff.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Inference stack.} llama.cpp at commit \texttt{5aba5364} (server build
    9456), native \texttt{/completion} endpoint, CUDA builds of the same commit per GPU
    architecture (sm\_75 for RTX 2080~Ti, sm\_120 for RTX 5070~Ti). Server-side BOS
    insertion is disabled; the client-rendered chat template owns the special tokens, and
    each record stores the SHA-256 of the exact rendered prompt.
  \item \textbf{Quantizations.} Every quantized checkpoint is a Bartowski imatrix GGUF
    (one provider, one recipe, so the quantization axis is not confounded by recipe).
    SHA-256 hashes of all 29 model files are pinned in the repository
    (\texttt{DOWNLOADS.md} and per-file \texttt{.meta.json} sidecars).
  \item \textbf{Decoding.} Exactly one truncation parameter per request; the sampler
    chain order is pinned per arm and recorded in every record. Sampler values: top-$p$
    0.95, top-$k$ 40, min-$p$ 0.05, top-$n\sigma$ 1.0 (each method's recommended value).
  \item \textbf{Seeding.} Every generation's seed is derived deterministically from
    (global seed 0, model, quantization, sampler, temperature, task, item, repetition).
    Greedy decoding is the deterministic $T{=}0$ anchor with $K{=}1$.
  \item \textbf{Tasks.} GSM8K (openai/gsm8k, test split, first 50 items) and MMLU-Pro
    (TIGER-Lab/MMLU-Pro, test split, first 50 items; stratified replication in
    Appendix~\ref{app:strat}). Parsers mirror lm-eval-harness; parse failures grade as
    incorrect and are reported.
  \item \textbf{Records.} Each generation is one JSONL record carrying the full identity
    tuple, the request parameters, the raw output, the parse path, and throughput
    covariates; runs are resumable by record id. Batched serving ($\le 8$ slots) is
    seed-logged but not bit-exact.

\end{itemize}
